01CalSol Solar Vehicle Team · ChassisFall 2026

Composite lower occupant cell

Designed and simulated a carbon-fiber / aluminum-honeycomb sandwich structure for the driver's lower occupant cell, then checked it against five 10 kN impact cases in Ansys.

What I did

  • Modeled the cell in SolidWorks and repaired un-knit surfaces in Ansys Discovery with manual bonded connections.
  • Researched ply orientation (0° / 90° / ±45°) and chose a symmetric [0 / 45 / core / 45 / 0] layup of NT-350HT woven prepreg over ¾" 5052 honeycomb.
  • Ran front, rear and three side impacts (10 kN on a 600 × 100 mm patch) and evaluated ply failure with Max Stress and Puck criteria.
  • Delivered a design 25% lighter than a 3-ply-per-side alternative (6.40 vs. 8.51 kg) while keeping minimum FoS at 2.0 or above.
  • Traced peak deformation to an unsupported front flange and proposed added structure and weight cuts for the next iteration.

03ENGIN 29 · Manufacturing & Design CommunicationSpring 2026My concept

The Strapper

A clamp-on organizer that turns the water bottle you already own into a gym carry system — magnetic phone mount, carabiner and swappable attachments, so nothing ends up on the gym floor.

Engineering highlights

  • Specified 5 fits per ASME B4.1: RC4 running fit on the hinge pin, LC2 locational clearance on the dowels, a snap-fit latch and a compliant bottle clamp.
  • Applied GD&T per ASME Y14.5 from three functional datums — cylindricity on the bottle bore, position on pin holes, perpendicularity on the phone platform.
  • Redesigned the latch after the first version cracked; tuned clamp tolerance to fit Ø84–86 mm bottles.
  • Selected PLA/FDM for prototyping and outlined a move to injection molding for production.

My role

The Strapper started as my idea — I pitched the concept to the team. From there we all worked across the whole project: CAD, prototyping and iteration, tolerancing and GD&T, and the final documentation.

04ME 100 · Electronics for the Internet of ThingsSpring 2026 · Team of 3

StudyShield

An ESP32 phone dock that notices when you pick up your phone mid-study session — and calls you out with an alarm and a push notification.

How it works

  • Force-sensitive resistor detects the phone's weight; a capacitance plate confirms it's in the dock.
  • Capacitive touch button toggles sessions; a 3 W amplified speaker plays four cues: on, off, ready and alarm.
  • ESP32 sends low-latency push alerts through NTFY, an open-source HTTP notification service.
  • Tuned firmware thresholds around noisy sensors and packaged all electronics into one printed housing.

My role

  • Drew the system circuit diagram used for assembly.
  • Wired the full system: ESP32, FSR, both capacitive sensors, amplifier and speaker.
  • Modeled the SolidWorks enclosure with an iPhone-sized cradle that aligns the phone over the force sensor.
  • Helped write the firmware.

ESP32 · FSR & capacitive sensing · Audio amp · NTFY · 3D-printed housing
